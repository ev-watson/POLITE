unobservable from Julian and excluded.

Planning Notes

Elevation cuts. Julian sits at 33.08° N. The 42° floor translates to a hard declination minimum of roughly -15°. All targets are within that
bound except z Phe (Dec -55°, excluded), and four targets that transit marginally above 42° and require tight scheduling near the meridian.

Unpolarized standards. The list draws from Schmidt, Elston & Lupie (1992, AJ 104, 1563) and Turnshek et al. (1990, AJ 99, 1243), the
two workhorse catalogs for optical polarimetric calibration. g Boo and BD+32 3739 are the strongest choices for a Julian session: both
transit near zenith with fractional polarization £0.07% and small uncertainties.

Polarized standards. There are fewer than 30 stars in both hemispheres with polarization degree known to 0.1% accuracy or better and

proven stable in time, so the list above represents most viable northern targets. Hiltner 960 (transit ~96° alt, PA 54.8°±0.1°) and BD+59 389

(6.70%, PA 98.1°) are the highest-priority EVPA calibrators. For multi-PA coverage across a session, pair two stars with PA offset by

30–45° (e.g., Hiltner 960 at 54.8° and HD 19820 at 115.4°).

Vega. Intrinsic linear polarization ~0.01–0.02% at optical wavelengths due to rapid rotation and near-pole-on geometry, making it effectively

unpolarized for most purposes but not a formal standard. Its primary value is as a simultaneous photometric and polarimetric flux anchor.

Heavy neutral density or defocusing required in polarimetric mode.

e Aurigae. Currently out of eclipse (next eclipse ~2036). Polarization is significant and variable only during eclipse events; in quiescence it
is not useful as a polarimetric standard. Flag as a monitoring/science target if tracking inter-eclipse behavior.

Eclipsing binaries. Algol and VW Cep are the highest-value targets for a single-session run: Algol for its large amplitude and canonical
status; VW Cep for its 6.7-hr period (full light curve in one night). b Lyr is scientifically rich due to disk-related intrinsic polarization but
requires ephemeris planning given its 12.9-day period. For any EB science, retrieve the current ephemeris from the AAVSO VSX or TIDAK

(Timing Database at Krakow) database before the run.
